## Source Summary

Pennock describes the Scientific Virtues Project as a philosophical and historical investigation of the relationship between epistemic and ethical values in science as they come together in the character of the exemplary scientist. He says his account centers on curiosity, veracity, and related character virtues whose normative force comes from their relation to the aims and methods of science.

The project page connects the theory to:

- a national empirical study of 1000+ scientists about virtues in exemplary scientific practice;
- virtue-based responsible conduct of research training;
- the VERITIES Initiative at Michigan State University;
- STEM education that teaches scientific values and virtues, not only content, methods, and practices;
- a broader vocational virtues framework for disciplined practices such as science, engineering, medicine, and the humanities.

MIT Press metadata for _An Instinct for Truth_ identifies curiosity, veracity, attentiveness, humility to evidence, and skepticism as central virtues in Pennock's account. The book table of contents also foregrounds utter honesty, systematic curiosity, ardent observation, scientific discipline, courageous humility, creative conflict, and scientific vices.

## ClaimWright Interpretation

For ClaimWright, scientific virtues are not decorative values. They are operational constraints on human-agent research collaboration.

They require agents to:

- challenge confirmation bias;
- report uncertainty and source access limits;
- preserve provenance;
- distinguish observation from interpretation;
- revise claims when evidence warrants;
- treat adversarial review as productive scientific discipline;
- maintain responsible conduct standards in public-facing and private research work.
